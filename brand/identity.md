# Research Desk Visual Identity Brief

## 01 - Identity Strategy Statement

Research Desk should look like a rigorous editorial instrument built for technical work, not an AI spectacle. The identity combines the authority of a research journal with the clarity of a well-designed developer console: quiet foundations, explicit structure, and restrained signals that show when evidence is moving through the system. Every visual decision should reinforce a visible method from question to source to report. The result must feel trustworthy enough for research teams and exact enough for engineers.

## 02 - Logo Direction

**Primary direction**: A symbol plus wordmark. The symbol is a compact desk mark in which three research paths converge into a document edge, while the wordmark uses a sober grotesk with a monospaced descriptor.

**Character**: Investigative, composed, systematic, and quietly confident.

**Style notes**: Constructed geometry, squared proportions, rounded only where needed for legibility. The mark should remain clear at favicon size and work in one color. Use asymmetry in the evidence paths to keep it from feeling corporate or static.

**What to avoid**: Robot heads, brains, magnifying glasses, sparkle stars, chat bubbles, orbit motifs, neural-network spheres, rainbow gradients, and literal desk furniture.

**3 Logo References**:

1. **Linear**: Borrow the disciplined geometry and ability to feel premium without decorative detail.
2. **The New York Times**: Borrow the editorial authority and confidence of a strong wordmark, not its blackletter styling.
3. **IBM**: Borrow the sense of systems thinking and technical permanence created through repetition and precise spacing.

## 03 - Color Palette

**Primary color**: Archive Ink, `#0B1220`. A blue-black foundation that signals depth, concentration, and technical credibility without defaulting to pure black.

**Secondary colors**:

- Signal Cyan, `#45C7E8`: Active states, focus borders, links, and research in progress.
- Evidence Mint, `#72E0B8`: Verified evidence, completion states, and sparse highlights.
- Paper, `#F4F1E8`: Warm light background and primary text on dark surfaces.
- Slate, `#8EA0B8`: Secondary text, metadata, and inactive controls.

**Color personality**: Nocturnal editorial workspace with precise instrumental signals. Calm dominates; color appears only when the system has something meaningful to communicate.

**Usage rules**: Archive Ink should occupy 70-80% of dark interfaces. Use cyan for actions and current activity, mint for evidence and success, and never use both as large competing fills. Paper and Ink are the only approved long-form text pairings.

**Palette type**: Restrained analogous cyan-green accents over a neutral blue-black and warm-paper base.

## 04 - Typography

**Primary typeface**: IBM Plex Sans. Its engineered construction feels technical, while its humanist details keep long documentation readable. Use SemiBold for display text and Regular for body copy.

**Secondary typeface**: IBM Plex Mono. Use it for run IDs, node names, commands, metadata, labels, and code-adjacent UI.

**Type personality**: Editorial hierarchy with terminal precision. The system should feel written and engineered in equal measure.

**Usage hierarchy**:

- Headlines: IBM Plex Sans SemiBold, tight tracking, sentence case.
- Subheads: IBM Plex Sans Medium, sentence case.
- Body: IBM Plex Sans Regular with generous line height.
- Labels: IBM Plex Mono Medium, uppercase, increased tracking.

**Avoid**: Geometric display sans fonts with circular novelty forms, sci-fi extended fonts, handwriting, serif body copy in product UI, and more than two font families.

## 05 - Imagery & Photography Style

**Overall aesthetic**: Documentary and analytical. Favor real interface states, architecture fragments, source excerpts, and close crops of working research artifacts over lifestyle photography.

**Subject matter**: Research plans, evidence trails, highlighted source material, terminal workflows, report structures, and people actively reviewing information when human imagery is necessary.

**Color treatment**: Low-saturation natural color with deep ink shadows and selective cyan or mint annotation. Diagrams should use flat color and high-contrast labels.

**What to avoid**: Floating holograms, humanoid robots, generic teams pointing at screens, glowing AI brains, neon cyberpunk scenes, and decorative data visualizations with no informational role.

## 06 - Iconography & Illustration

**Style**: Open geometric line icons built on a 16 or 24 pixel grid, with squared terminals and occasional filled status dots.

**Weight**: Regular, approximately 1.5 pixels at 24 pixels.

**Personality**: Primarily functional. Brand character comes from path-and-node compositions, editorial rules, and evidence annotations rather than illustrative mascots.

## 07 - Design Principles

**Show the method**  
Structure should reveal progression: question, plan, action, evidence, evaluation, report. Use ordered stages and explicit state changes rather than opaque loading experiences.

**Signal, do not decorate**  
Cyan and mint indicate action or verified evidence; they are not ambient decoration. If an element carries no information, it should remain neutral or be removed.

**Editorial before dashboard**  
Prioritize readable narrative, clear headings, and generous text rhythm over dense card grids. Reports are the product, so long-form reading quality must guide the layout.

**Technical details stay legible**  
Run IDs, tools, sources, and logs should be easy to scan in monospace without overwhelming the main narrative. Precision is a feature, but hierarchy keeps it usable.

## 08 - Brand Expressions

**GitHub and social profiles**: Use the square ink logo mark on a solid background. Social cards should pair one direct claim with a cropped workflow or report artifact, never a generic AI illustration.

**Website and application**: Lead with the question-to-report journey. Use Archive Ink as the workspace, Paper for report reading surfaces, cyan for active research, and mint for evidence completion.

**Terminal UI**: Preserve terminal density while creating an editorial split between the run ledger and report canvas. Labels use mono uppercase; report content gets the widest reading area.

**Documentation and README**: Open with the adaptive wordmark, one-line value proposition, proof-oriented badges, and a concise workflow. Installation and operations content should be scannable before conceptual detail.

**Presentations and demos**: Use a 12-column grid, large Paper headlines on Ink, one workflow idea per slide, and annotated product captures. Avoid feature mosaics and unsubstantiated AI claims.
